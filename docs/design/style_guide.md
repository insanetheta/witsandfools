# Wits and Fools: Visual Style Guide

## The Creative Vision

**One-line pitch:** "Climb the candlelit social ladder of Renaissance Italy through the treachery and triumph of a card game."

**Tone:** Warm, roguish, tactile. Think the golden hour glow of a Caravaggio painting mixed with the playful menace of a heist film. Not grim-dark. Not cartoon-bright. The world is dangerous but beautiful — you're a charming outsider who wins through wit, not violence.

**Feeling:** Imagine you're sitting at a real table. You can smell the tallow candles. The cards are thick, hand-pressed linen. The opponent across from you has a scar and a half-smile. Outside the window, the world gets richer and more dangerous with every venue you enter.

## Reference Touchstones

| Reference | What to Borrow |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Slay the Spire** | Illustrated character art, atmospheric act backgrounds, iconic silhouettes |
| **Inscryption** | Tactile, physical-feeling card play, environmental storytelling, tension |
| **Balatro** | Bold ability iconography, satisfying visual feedback, "one more run" compulsion |
| **Pentiment** | Renaissance setting done right — woodcut aesthetic, period typography |
| **Hades** | Character portraits with personality, progression that feels earned |

**What to AVOID:**
- Generic fantasy art (orcs, elves, glowing swords)
- Hyper-realistic 3D rendering (wrong medium for a card game)
- Flat mobile-game UI (Wits and Fools should feel premium and atmospheric)
- Pixel art (wrong tone for the Renaissance setting)

## The World: Renaissance Circuit

The game takes place across five venues, each representing a tier of Italian Renaissance society. The visual journey IS the story — you see and feel yourself climbing from the gutters to the palace.

### Act 1: The Bilge Rat Tavern

**Setting:** A waterfront dive. Warped wooden planks, guttering tallow candles, rope coils, barnacle-crusted porthole windows. Rats scurry in the corners. The card table is a repurposed barrel lid.

**Color palette:** Muddy browns, sickly yellows, desaturated greens, warm orange from candlelight. Dark with pools of warm light.

**Card table:** Rough wood surface with stains and knife marks. Cards look slightly dog-eared.

**Opponents:** Dock workers, fishmongers, retired sailors. Weathered faces, rough hands, missing teeth. They play aggressively and sloppily.
- **Barnacle Bill** — Ruddy-faced old sailor, missing an eye, perpetual grin
- **Salty Pete** — Wiry dockworker, nervous energy, twitchy hands
- **Dock Rat** — Young pickpocket, quick eyes, plays fast and loose
- **Fishy Meg** — Tavern regular, shrewd, deceptively smart beneath the grime

**Sound:** Creaking wood, distant sea, raucous laughter, sloshing ale

---

### Act 2: The Merchant's Rest

**Setting:** A prosperous trading inn. Polished oak furniture, brass fittings, maps and ledgers on shelves, scales and coin purses on side tables. Fireplace with carved mantel. Multi-paned glass windows showing a busy market square.

**Color palette:** Rich warm browns, brass/copper metallics, deep reds, cream/parchment tones. Well-lit, comfortable.

**Card table:** A proper game table with green baize surface (higher quality felt). Cards are cleaner.

**Opponents:** Traders, guild apprentices, traveling merchants. Well-dressed but not noble. They're calculating — they count cards and protect their position.
- **Merchant Luca** — Portly, jovial, rings on every finger, hides cunning behind laughter
- **Trader Yun** — Foreign merchant, calm and precise, says little
- **Silk Marco** — Young dandy, overdressed for his station, desperate to prove himself
- **Coin Bianca** — Sharp-eyed money changer, never misses a trick

**Sound:** Clinking coins, quill on parchment, polite murmur, fireplace crackle

---

### Act 3: The Guildmaster's Hall

**Setting:** A grand hall belonging to a powerful trade guild. High ceilings with dark wooden beams, oil portraits on walls, stained glass windows, iron chandeliers with real candles. A sense of authority and tradition.

**Color palette:** Deep burgundy, dark wood, gold leaf accents, midnight blue, ivory. Dramatic lighting from overhead.

**Card table:** An ornate table with carved legs, thick green felt, silver card tray. The deck has a tooled leather case.

**Opponents:** Guild masters, minor nobles, professional card players. Serious, skilled, with real stakes. They have abilities and use them well.
- **Guildmaster Voss** — Barrel-chested, iron-grey beard, plays like he runs a business
- **Lady Ashton** — First genuine noble you face, elegant and merciless
- **Baron Kell** — Military bearing, aggressive but disciplined, scars under fine clothes
- **Fixer Tomas** — Slippery, always smiling, deals from angles you don't expect

**Sound:** Echoing stone, distant organ music, servants' footsteps, clinking crystal

---

### Act 4: The Cardinal's Library

**Setting:** A private library within a cathedral complex. Floor-to-ceiling bookshelves, illuminated manuscripts under glass, astronomical instruments, a globe in the corner. Candlelight gleams off gold-tooled book spines. The table sits in an alcove surrounded by knowledge and secrets.

**Color palette:** Deep purples, ecclesiastical gold, crimson, dark emerald, aged parchment. Candlelight creates dramatic shadows.

**Card table:** Ebony table with mother-of-pearl inlay. Cards are thick, hand-painted. A crystal decanter of wine sits to one side.

**Opponents:** Church officials, spymasters, scholars. Brilliant, calculating, politically dangerous. They know your abilities and plan around them.
- **Cardinal Enzo** — Soft-spoken, devastating, plays three moves ahead
- **Sister Agatha** — Austere, patient, waits for you to overextend then strikes
- **Spymaster Grey** — Hooded, watchful, seems to know your hand before you play it
- **Scholar Ruiz** — Elderly, eccentric, plays unconventional strategies that catch you off guard

**Sound:** Turning pages, distant chanting, a clock ticking, hushed intensity

---

### Act 5: The Duke's Salon

**Setting:** The private gaming room of a Venetian duke. Gilt-framed mirrors, Murano glass chandeliers, silk wallpaper, marble floor. A balcony overlooks moonlit canals. This is the pinnacle — only the best players are invited here.

**Color palette:** Royal purple, Venetian gold, midnight blue, silver, pure white marble. Opulent, luminous, intoxicating.

**Card table:** A masterpiece. Mahogany with gold filigree, velvet playing surface, crystal card holders. The deck is hand-painted with gold leaf edges.

**Opponents:** The Duke's inner circle and the reigning champion.
- **The Champion** — The final boss. Masked (Venetian carnival mask), inscrutable, legendary. Their reputation precedes them. They have a full loadout and a powerful house rule.

**Sound:** String quartet, silk rustling, canal water lapping, the quiet intensity of a high-stakes game

## Character Design Principles

### The Player

Never shown directly. The player IS the player. Hands occasionally visible placing cards. This is first-person — the world is what you see sitting at the table.

### Opponents

Every opponent should be instantly readable from their portrait:
- **Silhouette test:** Can you identify them from their outline alone?
- **One visual hook:** Each needs ONE defining feature (Barnacle Bill's eyepatch, Cardinal Enzo's folded hands, The Champion's mask)
- **Archetype coding:** Brawlers look aggressive (leaning forward, broad shoulders). Misers look guarded (arms close, narrow eyes). Foxes look amused (half-smile, relaxed posture).
- **Progression coding:** Act 1 opponents should look rough and approachable. Act 5 opponents should look intimidating and refined.

### Portrait Format

Half-bust portraits (chest up), seated at their side of the table. Consistent framing, varied poses. Think poker player photos but painted in oil.

## The Cards

### Face Design

Cards should feel hand-illustrated in a Renaissance woodcut/engraving style:
- **Number cards (6-10):** Suit symbols arranged in traditional patterns, framed by delicate vine borders
- **Court cards (J, Q, K, A):** Illustrated characters in Renaissance dress, different for each suit
  - Spades: Military/martial figures
  - Hearts: Romantic/artistic figures
  - Diamonds: Mercantile/wealthy figures
  - Clubs: Scholarly/religious figures

### Card Back Design

An ornate repeating pattern featuring:
- Central emblem: A jester's cap (the "Fool" of the title) intertwined with a fox (the "Wit")
- Border: Renaissance scrollwork and vine patterns
- Colors: Deep crimson and gold on dark background
- Subtle texture suggesting pressed linen

### Ability Badge

Cards carrying abilities should show:
- A small heraldic shield in the bottom-left corner
- The ability icon inside the shield
- A subtle colored glow around the card edge matching ability type:
  - Attack abilities: Warm red/orange
  - Defense abilities: Cool blue/silver
  - Utility abilities: Gold/amber
  - Passive abilities: Soft green

## Environmental Storytelling

### The Table is the World

The core insight: players spend 60%+ of their time looking at the card table. The table surface, the objects around it, the light quality — these ARE the game's visual world.

**Per-venue table variations:**
- Act 1: Barrel lid, spilled ale stain, knife marks, guttering candle
- Act 2: Green baize on oak, brass tack trim, coin purse to one side
- Act 3: Carved oak with guild crest inlay, silver candlestick, leather deck case
- Act 4: Ebony with pearl inlay, crystal wine glass, scattered book pages
- Act 5: Gold filigree on mahogany, velvet surface, Murano glass candlestick

**Ambient detail layers:**
1. Table surface (primary focus)
2. Table edge objects (candle, drink, trinkets — changes per venue)
3. Background blur (suggests the room beyond the table)
4. Lighting quality (warm/yellow for tavern, cool/white for library)

### Progression Visualization

The player should FEEL the climb:
- Cards get physically nicer (dog-eared -> crisp -> gold-edged)
- The table gets more ornate
- The lighting gets more sophisticated
- The background sounds shift from rowdy to refined
- Even the HUD could evolve (rough chalk marks -> engraved metalwork)

## Animation & Feedback

### Card Animations
- **Play:** Cards slide from hand to table with a satisfying snap
- **Defense:** Defense card drops onto attack with a covering motion
- **Take cards:** Reluctant drag back to hand, cards looking heavy
- **Discard:** Cards sweep to discard pile with a flourish
- **Deal:** Cards dealt one at a time with a crisp snap sound
- **Ability activation:** Brief flash/glow, card rises slightly before playing

### Screen Transitions
- **Map to match:** Camera push into the table surface, dissolve from map
- **Match to result:** Table fades, result text resolves from candlelight glow
- **Between acts:** A brief interstitial showing travel (horse and carriage silhouette against changing backgrounds)
- **Run over (win):** Slow zoom out from table, revealing the grand salon around you
- **Run over (loss):** Opponent's hand sweeps the cards, screen darkens

### Juice Moments
- Winning a bout: Cards sweep to discard with a satisfying "whoosh"
- Perfect defense: Brief golden glow on the table
- Ability activation: Unique visual per ability (Trump Changer shows suit symbols swirling)
- Prestige loss: Screen edge briefly flashes red
- Florins gained: Coins briefly cascade on screen
- Level up to new act: Dramatic venue transition with title card

## Narrative Voice

### Text Tone
Renaissance-flavored but not Shakespearean. Warm, wry, slightly ironic. The narrator is an amused observer, not a stuffy historian.

**Good:** "The hearth crackles. You find a quiet moment of peace."
**Better:** "The fire pops. For once, nobody's trying to cheat you."

**Good:** "Dock Rat bested you."
**Better:** "The little wretch grins as you push back from the barrel. You won't underestimate dock rats again."

### Opponent Voice Lines
Each opponent should have 2-3 short lines shown during the match:
- On first attack: "Let's see what you've got." / "Draw steel, friend."
- On ability use: "Clever trick." / "I know that one."
- On bout win: "Too slow." / "The tide turns."
- On defeat: "Well played..." / "Next time, perhaps."

Lines should reflect archetype and act level. Tavern opponents are crude and direct. Library opponents are subtle and poetic.
