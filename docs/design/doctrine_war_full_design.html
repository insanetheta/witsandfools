RULES OF PLAY

Rules of Play

Durak combat with per-player decks and ability-triggering cards.

⚔
Attack & Defend
The attacker plays cards face-up. The defender must beat each attack card with a card of the same suit and higher rank, or a trump card. Multiple attack cards can be played if they share a rank with cards already on the table.
🃏
Trump Suit
One suit is designated trump at match start (randomly, unless modified by relics). Trump cards beat any non-trump card regardless of suit. Among trump cards, higher rank wins. Trump is the great equalizer.
🐟
Eating Cards
If the defender cannot (or chooses not to) beat all attacks, they "eat" — picking up every card on the table into their hand. The attacker then gets another turn. Eating is punishing but can fuel Hoarder strategies.
🏆
Win Condition
Empty your hand when your draw pile is exhausted. The first player to have zero cards wins. The last player holding cards is the "Durak" (fool) and loses the match.
📜
Per-Player Decks
Unlike traditional Durak which uses a shared 36-card deck, each player brings their own constructed deck (12–25 cards). Your deck is your character build, shaped by doctrine choices throughout the run.
🔄
Draw & Discard
At the end of each bout, players draw from their own deck to refill to their hand limit (default 6). Successfully defended cards are removed from the game. Cards eaten go to the defender's hand.

Ability Triggers

Every doctrine card has an ability that fires at a specific timing:

⚔
OnAttack
Triggers when this card is played as an attack. Effects resolve before the defender responds. Brute cards often have OnAttack triggers for rank boosts and pressure.
🛡
OnDefend
Triggers when this card is played to defend against an attack. Effects resolve after the defense is accepted. Schemer and Hoarder cards favor OnDefend for card draw and resource gain.
💡
Passive
Always active while in your hand or in play. Provides continuous bonuses like increased hand size, rank boosts, or triggered effects. Powerful but takes a deck slot.
✖
Active (None)
Can be activated by discarding the card from your hand as a free action. Provides a one-time powerful effect at the cost of losing the card. Sacrifice for immediate impact.

Deck Cycling

When your draw pile empties, there is no reshuffle. Cards played in a successful defense are removed from the game entirely — neither player gets them back. This creates natural attrition: decks get thinner as the match progresses, making every card increasingly precious. The Schemer's card draw and the Hoarder's pickup benefits become more valuable as the draw pile shrinks.

THE FOUR DOCTRINES

The Four Doctrines

Each doctrine defines a philosophy of play, a resource, and a starting deck of 12 cards.

👁
The Schemer
"Knowledge is the sharpest blade."
Resource: Intel
Card draw, deck manipulation, information warfare. Schemers see more cards, cycle faster, and always know what's coming. They win through inevitability — grinding opponents down with card advantage and perfect information.
Draw Scry Reveal Cycle Intel

⚔
The Brute
"Overwhelming force needs no subtlety."
Resource: Fury
Raw numbers, forced pick-ups, relentless pressure. Brutes pump their cards' effective ranks, attack with multiple cards, and punish failed defenses. They win by making every bout a losing proposition for the defender.
+Rank Multi-attack Punish Force pickup Fury

🃏
The Trickster
"The rules are just suggestions."
Resource: Favor
Trump manipulation, suit-shifting, rule-bending. Tricksters change the trump suit mid-bout, make cards count as different suits, and invalidate the opponent's carefully planned defense. They win by making the game play by their rules.
Trump shift Wild suit Redirect Nullify Favor

💰
The Hoarder
"The one with the most cards wins... eventually."
Resource: Luck
Benefits from large hands, long games, and attrition. Hoarders gain power the more cards they hold, turn failed defenses (picking up cards) into advantages, and outlast opponents. They win by flipping Durak's core rule on its head.

Act Structure Overview

🍺
Act I — The Bilge Rat Tavern
The dockside slums. Low-stakes games against drunks and thugs. Brute-heavy enemies teach the core attack/defend loop. Boss: Il Padrone.
⛵
Act II — The Merchant's Rest
The trading quarter. All four doctrines appear. Mid-stakes games against traders and hustlers. Boss: Il Capitano.
🏢
Act III — The Guild Hall
The guild district. High-synergy enemies with nearly full doctrine decks. Boss: Il Maestro.
⛩
Act IV — The Cardinal's Court
The ecclesiastical quarter. Enemies with house rules and near-zero random play. Boss: Il Cardinale.
👑
Act V — The Doge's Palace
The seat of power. Perfect-play opponents with pure doctrine decks. The final boss is the only hybrid-doctrine enemy in the game. Boss: Il Doge.

DECK BUILDING

Deck Building Rules

The tension between lean efficiency and broad options defines the deckbuilder.

12
Starting Deck Size
Every doctrine begins with exactly 12 cards. All starting cards are Common or Uncommon rarity. This is the baseline.
10–16
Recommended Range
10–16 cards is the sweet spot. Small decks cycle fast (great for Schemers), large decks provide options (great for Hoarders).
18+
Warning Threshold
At 18+ cards, the game warns you that synergies are diluting. You'll draw your key combos less often. Only Hoarders should consider this.
25
Hard Cap
Maximum deck size. No deck can exceed 25 cards. This prevents infinite scaling and ensures every card matters.

Card Acquisition

🏆
Match Rewards
After winning a match, choose 1 of 3 offered cards (or skip). Cards are weighted toward the current act's difficulty and the player's doctrine.
💰
Shop Purchases
Buy specific cards from the shop. Common: 5–8F, Uncommon: 10–16F, Rare: 18–25F. Cross-doctrine cards cost +3F.

Card Removal

🔥
Rest Stops
At rest stops, you may choose to remove a card instead of healing. Free, but costs your healing opportunity. Strategic pruning.
💰
Shop Removal
Pay to remove a card. First removal: 5F. Each subsequent removal costs 5F more (10F, 15F, 20F...). Escalating cost creates real decisions.

The Tension

Small deck = fast cycle through your draw pile, see your best cards more often, stronger synergy chains. But fewer options when trump shifts or you face unexpected situations.

Big deck = more answers to varied threats, more flexibility in combat. But synergies fire less often, key cards get buried, and you're slower to empty your hand for the win.

The ideal deck size is a function of your doctrine, your relics, and the enemies you expect to face. There is no single right answer — and that's the point.

Prestige (Run HP)

You start each run with 3 Prestige. Losing a match costs 1 Prestige. Losing to a boss costs 2 Prestige. When Prestige hits 0, the run is over (unless you hold The Phoenix Medal). Rest stops can restore 1 Prestige. Some rumor events also heal Prestige.

Burdens

Burdens are negative modifiers gained from rumor events, elite defeats, or risky choices. Examples:

💨
Cursed Hand
Start each match with 1 fewer card. Can be removed at a rest stop or by paying 15F at a shop.
👀
Marked by the Inquisition
Enemies can see 1 random card in your hand at match start. Removed after defeating an elite.
💰
Debtor's Chain
Lose 2F after every match, win or lose. Removed by paying 20F at a shop.
